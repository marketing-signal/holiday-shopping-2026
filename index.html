Eight things to take away

The evidence comes from Signal data. The implications are our reading of it, so test them against what you know about your own business.

1

The peak is still ahead

Black Friday itself has not happened yet. Last year (actual data), Black Friday week ran 2.3× the week two weeks out and 1.5× the week before. If 2026 follows the same shape, 23 to 29 Nov will be the loudest stretch. This is last year's pattern, not a forecast.

2

Amazon sets the agenda

45,371 stories, 4× Walmart (11,323). Everyone else competes for what is left, so differentiation matters more than volume.

3

Price is the common language

"% off" shows up in 55,485 stories. Financing (2,535) and gifts (2,516) barely register, which leaves room to stand out.

4

Sentiment is hygiene, not a edge

Every brand tracked is under 9% negative. The risk sits in the stories around the sales: scams (7,418), economic pressure (13,164), worker safety.

5

The tail is long

Last year the week after Black Friday still ran at 55% of the peak week. Keep something in reserve for Cyber Monday and beyond.

6

Readership favours guides and "early", not "% off"

Percentage-off language has 3.7× the stories of early access, yet in a keyword scan of the readership sample, "early" articles drew 3.8× the total readership. Guides, dates and best-time explainers have the highest average readership.

8

Quieter than last year, with a different mix

On complete days coverage is 13% below 2025. About four-fifths of the net drop is broadcast, social is up 154%, and Amazon's lead over Walmart doubled (1.9× to 4.0×). Early-access language fell 47%.

7

Crossovers beat generic headlines

Using the report's tags: articles with Black Friday in the headline have a median readership of 968, against 1,374 where it is only in the body. Articles tagged with AI reach 3,159, 2.7× the sample median.

Coverage has climbed 5.7× since 1 November

Daily stories rose from 7,165 on 1 November to 40,533 on 17 November. Weekday volume has stepped up each week (13.8k to 21.9k, then 24.1k to 33.1k, then 37.3k to 40.5k) and every weekend has been busier than the last. Click any bar for detail.

How to read the gap

It is like for like. Each 2026 day is matched to the 2025 day with the same weekday and the same distance from Black Friday.

18 Nov 2026 is a part day. It has 32,933 stories against a full 51,013 last year, which on its own widens the gap. On complete days volume is 13.4% lower (405,112 against 468,054), not 15.6%.

Counts measure stories in Signal's index, not shopping or sales. If the sources monitored or the way entities are tagged changed between years, counts move without anything changing in the real world. Large swings for a single brand, country or phrase are worth confirming in the platform before acting.

Broadcast is about 70% of stories in both years, so the headline is mostly a broadcast story. Look at the non-broadcast figures (down 11.6%) for editorial and online coverage.

Coverage is warm. Negativity is thin and concentrated

No brand tops 9% negative. Temu (8.8%, on a small base), Shein (4.9%) and Apple (4.6%) carry the highest negative shares, and Apple has the most negative stories outright (747). Samsung has the warmest coverage at 63% positive, while Costco and Apple are mostly neutral.

Volume is not attention: what people actually read

From your readership report: 245 articles published 1 to 17 November, with 929,339 total readership between them. It is a sample of the 438,045 stories above, so use it to see what pulls attention, not to count coverage.

Most-read articles in the sample

Readership is the total readership figure in your report. The Tags view uses the report's Tags column. That column has no brand or deal-mechanic tags (and the Brand(s) column only holds EVENTS and TOPICS), so the two views marked "keyword scan" are our own scans of headlines and extracts. Signal story counts use a different match, so compare rankings, not raw numbers. The sample thins after 13 Nov (8 articles published 14 to 17 Nov), probably because readership accrues, so do not read a trend from it.

Where and how it is covered
